How to Add the 50 MA and 200 MA
📊
Why these two MAs? The 50 MA shows the medium-term trend and acts as a dynamic entry zone — price pulls back to it in trending markets. The 200 MA shows the long-term trend and is the most watched level by institutional traders worldwide. Together they form the backbone of the Blue Mac system.
On TradingView (Recommended)
1
Open your chart
Go to tradingview.com, search for your instrument (e.g. XAUUSD for Gold, EURUSD for Euro/Dollar) and select your timeframe using the toolbar at the top.
2
Add the 50 MA
Click the Indicators button at the top of the chart (or press / on your keyboard). Search for Moving Average. Click it to add it. Then double-click the MA line that appears → set Length = 50 · Source = Close · Type = Simple (SMA). Change the colour to something visible like yellow or orange.
3
Add the 200 MA
Repeat the same process. Add another Moving Average indicator. This time set Length = 200 · Source = Close · Type = Simple (SMA). Use a different colour — white or light blue works well so you can clearly distinguish it from the 50 MA.
4
Save as a template
Once both MAs are set up, click the Templates button (looks like a layout icon) → Save as Template. Name it "Blue Mac Setup". Now you can apply these MAs to any chart instantly without repeating the setup.
On MT4 / MT5
1
Open a chart
In MT4/MT5 right-click any instrument in the Market Watch panel → Chart Window. Then select your timeframe from the toolbar (M15 = 15 minutes, H1 = 1 hour, H4 = 4 hours, D1 = Daily).
2
Add the 50 MA
Click Insert → Indicators → Trend → Moving Average. Set Period = 50 · Method = Simple · Apply to = Close. Choose a visible colour. Click OK.
3
Add the 200 MA
Repeat: Insert → Indicators → Trend → Moving Average. This time set Period = 200. Use a different colour from the 50 MA so you can clearly tell them apart at a glance.
Trading Session Times
🕐
All times shown in UTC (Universal Time). To find your local equivalent — search "UTC to [your city] time converter" online. South Africa (SAST) is UTC+2 in winter and UTC+2 year-round (no daylight saving).
🇬🇧 London Open PRIME
08:00 – 12:00 UTC · 10:00 – 14:00 SAST
The most important session of the day. Highest volume, strongest directional moves. The first hour (08:00–09:00 UTC) is the best scalp and day trade window. Most major breakouts happen here.
🔥 London/NY Overlap PRIME
13:00 – 16:00 UTC · 15:00 – 18:00 SAST
Peak global liquidity. Both London and New York are active simultaneously. This produces the largest intraday moves of the entire day. The ideal window for day trading and high-quality scalps.
🇺🇸 New York Session
13:00 – 21:00 UTC · 15:00 – 23:00 SAST
Strong session particularly for USD pairs and US indices (US30, NAS100, SPX500). The open (13:00–15:00 UTC) overlaps with London for maximum volatility. Quieter after 17:00 UTC.
🌏 Asian Session
00:00 – 07:00 UTC · 02:00 – 09:00 SAST
Low volume, tight ranges, frequent false breakouts. Avoid scalping and day trading during Asian hours. JPY pairs are slightly more active. Swing and position traders can ignore session times entirely.
🇦🇺 Sydney Session
22:00 – 07:00 UTC · 00:00 – 09:00 SAST
Very low volume for most instruments. AUD and NZD pairs have slightly more activity. Not recommended for Blue Mac strategies — wait for London to open.
🇿🇦 SAST Quick Guide
UTC + 2 hours (year-round)
South Africa does not observe daylight saving. London Open = 10:00 SAST. London/NY Overlap = 15:00 SAST. This means South African traders get the best sessions in the afternoon — ideal for after-work trading.

The High-Impact Events to ALWAYS Avoid
These events regularly move markets 100–500 points and must never be traded through:

NFP — Non-Farm Payrolls (First Friday of every month, 13:30 UTC)
CPI — Consumer Price Index / Inflation data
FOMC — Federal Reserve meetings and rate decisions
Interest Rate Decisions — Any central bank (ECB, BOE, RBA, etc.)
GDP — Gross Domestic Product releases
✅
The 30-Minute Rule
For scalp and day trading — do not open a new trade within 30 minutes before OR after a high-impact news event. The 30 minutes before = spreads widen and liquidity dries up. The 30 minutes after = volatility spikes unpredictably. Wait for the dust to settle and a clean structure to form before looking for your entry.

Price Position vs MAs
MA
On your chart you will see two curved lines following price. The 50 MA (faster, closer to price) and the 200 MA (slower, further away). Where price sits relative to these lines tells you the trend direction.
Bullish
Bearish
💡
The rule: Only take long (buy) trades when price is above both MAs. Only take short (sell) trades when price is below both MAs. Between the MAs = no trade zone.
📐
MA Stack — 50 vs 200
MA
The MA Stack tells you whether the trend is confirmed. When the 50 MA is above the 200 MA, buyers are in control. When the 50 MA is below the 200 MA, sellers are in control.
50 above 200
50 below 200
💡
The rule: Bullish stack = 50 MA above 200 MA → look for longs. Bearish stack = 50 MA below 200 MA → look for shorts. Tangled MAs = no clear trend → wait.
🎯
The 50 MA Pullback Entry
MA · ENTRY
The 50 MA acts like a magnet. In an uptrend, price moves up, pulls back down to the 50 MA, then bounces up again — that pullback is your buy zone. In a downtrend, price falls, rallies up to the 50 MA, then rejects — that rally is your sell zone.
Buy the pullback
Sell the rally
⚠️
If price is already far from the 50 MA — the entry has passed. Do not chase. Wait for price to pull back to the 50 MA before entering. Chasing extended moves is the most common trading mistake.
🏔
Swing High & Swing Low
STRUCTURE
A Swing High is a candle whose high is higher than both the candle before it and after it — a visible peak. A Swing Low is a candle whose low is lower than both neighbours — a visible trough. These define where the market has turned.
💡
How to find them: Zoom out your chart. Look for obvious peaks (swing highs) and obvious troughs (swing lows). The most recent ones are the most important. Your SL for longs goes just below the most recent swing low. SL for shorts goes just above the most recent swing high.
📈
Market Structure — HH/HL vs LL/LH
STRUCTURE
Markets move in waves. An uptrend makes Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL) — each peak and each trough is higher than the last. A downtrend makes Lower Lows (LL) and Lower Highs (LH).
HH / HL
LL / LH
💡
The rule: Never fight the structure. If the market is making HH/HL, only look for long setups. If it's making LL/LH, only look for short setups. Structure breaks when a swing low is broken in an uptrend — that's your warning sign.
🎯
Support & Resistance
LEVELS
Support is a price below current price where the market has previously bounced up — the floor. Resistance is a price above current price where the market has previously turned down — the ceiling. The more times price has reacted at a level, the stronger it is.
💡
How to find key levels: Zoom out your chart 3–6 months. Look for horizontal zones where price reversed multiple times, had long wicks, or stalled. Previous day/week highs and lows, and round numbers are always key levels.

Spinning Top — Equal Indecision
CANDLES
A small body with equal upper and lower wicks. Bulls and bears fought hard but neither won — the candle closed roughly where it opened. Signals pure indecision. Similar to a doji but with a slightly larger body.
⚠️
What to do: A spinning top at a key level after a strong trend move = potential reversal warning. Do NOT enter on the spinning top itself. Wait for the next candle to close and show you the direction. The spinning top is a warning, not a signal.

CANDLES

How to trade it: A harami is not a strong standalone signal — it is a warning that momentum is slowing. Wait for the candle after the harami to confirm direction before entering. A harami followed by a pin bar or engulfing in the opposite direction is a high-probability reversal setup.

Push Count — Wave Exhaustion
STRUCTURE
Markets move in waves. Each directional move from a key level is called a "push". The 1st push has the most runway. By the 3rd push, the market is exhausted — most retail traders enter here and get trapped by the reversal.
⚠️
The trap: Most retail traders enter on the 3rd push because "the trend is obvious". This is exactly when institutions are distributing (selling into strength). If price has made 3+ pushes — wait for a pullback to a key level before entering.
